1. Project Introduction

CleanTech is a predictive green energy management and MRV platform. It is designed to collect energy data, measure renewable generation and consumption, prepare reports, verify data quality, and support operational decisions.

The platform can monitor renewable generation, electricity consumption, grid usage, battery storage, and other energy assets. Machine learning can be used to forecast future energy demand and renewable energy generation.

Meaning of MRV

  • Measurement: Collect and calculate energy and environmental information.
  • Reporting: Present processed information through dashboards and formal reports.
  • Verification: Check the source, quality, calculations, and supporting evidence.
Project Status: Features described as optional or proposed should be marked as implemented only after development and testing.

2. Problem Statement

Organizations may use solar energy, grid electricity, batteries, and multiple meters. Their information is often distributed across spreadsheets, devices, and separate monitoring tools.

  • Manual data collection and reporting take time.
  • Data may contain missing values, duplicate records, or incorrect units.
  • Renewable generation and consumption are difficult to compare in one place.
  • Future demand and renewable generation are difficult to estimate manually.
  • Battery and load-shifting decisions may not be data-driven.
  • Reports may lack traceable sources and verification records.

CleanTech addresses these issues by bringing data collection, analytics, forecasting, reporting, and decision support into one application.

3. Project Objectives

  • Collect renewable generation and energy consumption data.
  • Provide a centralized energy monitoring dashboard.
  • Calculate renewable matching, energy deficit, and grid dependency.
  • Generate structured and traceable MRV reports.
  • Forecast future energy demand and renewable generation.
  • Monitor battery status and energy storage information.
  • Suggest explainable load-shifting and energy-saving actions.
  • Detect missing, abnormal, or inconsistent readings.
  • Support future IoT integration and multi-site monitoring.

4. How the System Works

The system follows a data pipeline. Data enters the application, is validated and stored, is processed by the MRV engine, and is then used by analytics and machine learning components.

1
Data Input Readings arrive through CSV files, forms, APIs, smart meters, or optional IoT devices.
2
Validation The system checks timestamps, units, missing values, duplicates, and invalid readings.
3
Storage Validated data is stored with site, asset, timestamp, value, unit, and source information.
4
Processing The MRV engine calculates energy totals, renewable matching, grid usage, and environmental indicators.
5
Forecasting Machine learning models estimate future energy demand and renewable generation.
6
Output Dashboards, alerts, recommendations, and reports are displayed to the user.

5. System Architecture

The frontend communicates with the backend through HTTP or REST APIs. The backend manages authentication, validation, calculations, database operations, and communication with the ML layer.

Frontend
Dashboard, forms, charts, filters, alerts, and reports
↓ REST API
Backend
Authentication, business logic, MRV calculations, and report generation
↓ Database Operations
Database
Users, sites, assets, readings, forecasts, reports, and alerts
↔
Machine Learning Layer
Preprocessing, training, prediction, evaluation, and model storage
Security: Secret keys, database credentials, and model credentials must remain on the backend and must not be exposed in frontend code.

6. Main Modules

6.1 Data Collection

Receives readings from files, forms, APIs, meters, or IoT devices. Records should contain timestamp, site, asset, value, unit, and source.

6.2 Energy Monitoring

Displays renewable generation, consumption, grid import/export, battery status, totals, and historical trends.

6.3 MRV Calculation

Calculates energy indicators, prepares reports, records data sources, and supports verification.

6.4 ML Forecasting

Predicts future demand and renewable generation using historical data and optional weather features.

6.5 Battery Monitoring

Tracks state of charge, capacity, charging, discharging, operating limits, and battery alerts.

6.6 Recommendation System

Suggests load shifting, battery use, or energy-saving actions based on demand, generation, tariffs, and constraints.

6.7 Dashboard and Reports

Shows charts, summary cards, filters, site-wise results, and downloadable reports.

6.8 Data Quality and Alerts

Identifies missing readings, unusual values, delayed data, and possible sensor problems.

8. Database Design

The database stores raw measurements and processed results. A relational design can connect users, sites, assets, readings, forecasts, and reports.

Model Important Fields Purpose
User id, name, email, password_hash, role Authentication and access control.
Site id, name, location, capacity, owner_id Represents a building or industrial location.
EnergyAsset id, site_id, asset_type, capacity, status Solar panels, batteries, and meters.
EnergyReading id, asset_id, timestamp, value, unit, source Time-based energy measurements.
Forecast id, site_id, forecast_time, predicted_value, model_version Stores future predictions.
MRVReport id, site_id, period_start, period_end, status Stores report metadata and status.
Alert id, site_id, alert_type, severity, message Stores operational and data-quality alerts.
Recommendation id, site_id, action, reason, expected_impact Stores suggested actions and explanations.

Relationships

  • One user can manage multiple sites.
  • One site can contain multiple assets.
  • One asset can have multiple readings.
  • One site can have multiple forecasts, reports, alerts, and recommendations.

9. Machine Learning Workflow

1
Collect Data Gather historical demand, generation, weather, calendar, and site features.
2
Preprocess Data Handle missing values, duplicates, outliers, units, and timestamps.
3
Create Features Create hour, weekday, month, lag values, rolling averages, and weather features.
4
Split Data Use a time-based train and test split for chronological forecasting.
5
Train Model Start with a baseline model and compare algorithms such as Random Forest or Gradient Boosting.
6
Evaluate Model Use MAE, RMSE, and baseline comparison to measure prediction error.
7
Deploy and Monitor Save model versions, generate forecasts, monitor error, and retrain when required.
MAE = Average of Absolute Prediction Errors
RMSE = Square Root of Average Squared Prediction Errors
Important: Predictions are estimates. Critical equipment should not be controlled automatically without safety rules, testing, and human approval.

10. MRV Calculation

10.1 Measurement

Measurement collects energy values from approved sources. Each reading should include a timestamp, unit, asset, site, source, and data-quality status.

  • Renewable generation in kWh.
  • Total consumption in kWh.
  • Grid import and export.
  • Battery charging and discharging.
  • Data completeness and abnormal readings.

10.2 Reporting

Reporting converts measurements into daily, weekly, monthly, or site-wise summaries.

  • Total renewable generation and consumption.
  • Renewable matching percentage.
  • Grid dependency and energy deficit.
  • Estimated emissions avoided, when a valid emission factor is available.
  • Data sources, reporting period, and report generation time.

10.3 Verification

Verification checks whether reports are supported by valid data and reproducible calculations.

  • Check timestamps, units, missing values, and duplicates.
  • Compare calculated totals with source records.
  • Document calculation rules and emission-factor sources.
  • Store report versions and audit logs.
  • Show data coverage and validation status.

Example Formulas

Renewable Matching (%) = Renewable Energy Used ÷ Total Consumption × 100
Energy Deficit = Total Consumption − Renewable Energy Available
Estimated Emissions Avoided = Renewable Energy Displacing Grid Energy × Emission Factor

The final formulas must follow the project's selected energy boundary, storage rules, grid accounting method, and documented emission factor.

Final Project Definition

CleanTech is a proposed predictive green energy MRV platform that combines energy data collection, monitoring, reporting, verification, machine learning forecasting, and decision support to make renewable energy performance measurable and easier to improve.

Development Principle: Build and test the data pipeline and calculations first. Add forecasting and recommendations after the foundation is reliable.
